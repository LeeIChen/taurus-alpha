Moderna (MRNA): the cancer vaccine works, the price assumes it works everywhere

INTerpath-001 is a real scientific win. At $188.94 the market is paying for a pan-tumor blockbuster that the pipeline, the Merck profit split and the timelines cannot deliver on any reasonable probability. We rate the shares a Sell.

Summary

  • The melanoma result is genuine. On Aug 19, 2026 Merck and Moderna reported that the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial of intismeran plus Keytruda met its primary endpoint (recurrence-free survival) and key secondary endpoint (distant metastasis-free survival) at the first interim analysis. It is the first positive Phase 3 for an mRNA cancer therapy. The hazard ratio has not been disclosed.
  • The stock has priced in far more than melanoma. Shares rose from a 52-week low of $22.28 to $188.94, a ${{mcap:0}}B market cap. Every published sell-side target we found is below the price (Citi $80 Sell, consensus $121, high $135).
  • Our sum-of-the-parts DCF is ${{dcf}} per share today; 20x risk-adjusted 2032 EPS gives ${{pe20}}. Blending the 12-month DCF (${{dcf12}}) and the P/E value gives a ${{TARGET}} target.
  • Moderna keeps only half of intismeran. Costs and profits are split 50/50 with Merck worldwide, and individualized manufacturing keeps margins well below a typical antibody.
  • The legacy business is shrinking and the balance sheet is thinner than it looks. 2025 revenue was $1.94B with $1.87B of operating cash burn. Pro forma net cash is about ${{cash}} per share after the $3.0B convertible, with up to $1.3B more owed if a patent appeal is lost.

To justify ${{P}}, intismeran would need roughly ${{int_need:0}}B of global peak sales across tumor types, with every trial succeeding.

Our base case sums to ${{int_base:1}}B across all nine trial indications before risk adjustment. Keytruda, the best-selling drug in the world, sold $31.7B in 2025. Holding everything else at our base case, the market price requires intismeran to become several Keytrudas.

Valuation

Method 1: Sum-of-the-parts DCF (risk-adjusted NPV)

Each program is modelled from launch through 2045: sales ramp, peak, decline after exclusivity, a launch-to-peak margin ramp, and remaining development cost. Cash flows are multiplied by the program's probability of success. Development spending before the pivotal readout is counted in full, and spending after it is probability-weighted. Moderna books 50% of intismeran economics. We then subtract platform and corporate overhead (${{corp}} per share) and the expected cost of the Arbutus/Genevant contingency (${{lit}}), and add pro forma net cash (${{cash}}). Discount rate 10%. NOLs shield taxes through 2031, then 15%.

Total: ${{dcf}} per share today, or ${{dcf12}} rolled forward 12 months. With every program assumed to succeed (no risk adjustment) the same model gives ${{unrisked}}.

Method 2: P/E on risk-adjusted earnings

Probability-weighted operating income is still negative in 2028 (${{op28:0}}M), turns slightly positive in 2030 (${{op30:0}}M) and reaches ${{op32:0}}M in 2032 as flu, the combination vaccine and intismeran mature. We use 2032, the first year near steady state, because 2030 earnings are mostly launch costs. Risk-adjusted 2032 EPS of ${{pe_eps}} on 419M fully diluted shares, at 20x (a large-cap biotech with growth), discounted back to October 2027 at 10%, gives ${{pe20}}. At 25x it is ${{pe25}}.

How the cancer markets are sized

  • Adjuvant melanoma, $2.5B global peak. ACS projects 112,000 US melanomas in 2026. SEER shows 10% diagnosed at regional stage (stage III), and roughly 5,500 a year are stage IIB/C, so about 16,700 US patients a year fit the INTerpath-001 population. We assume 60% receive adjuvant Keytruda and 60% of those add intismeran at peak, at about $300K per course in the US (between a published $200K cost-effectiveness case and William Blair's $475K) and about $140K elsewhere.
  • Adjuvant NSCLC stage II–IIIB, $3.0B global peak. ACS projects 229,410 US lung cancers in 2026, 77% NSCLC. We assume about 15% are resected stage II–IIIB, 60% get adjuvant immunotherapy and 40% of those add intismeran.
  • Margins. Each course is manufactured for one patient (estimated production cost above $100K), so we use a 58% contribution margin at peak, after cost of goods and selling costs, before Moderna's half.

Probability of success: basis and sources

Probabilities start from the BIO / Informa / QLS study of 12,728 phase transitions from 2011 to 2020, then move up or down for program-specific evidence. Recommendation

Sell. The direction of the call holds up across methods. Our base case is ${{TARGET}}, our bull case is ${{BULL:0}}, and the most optimistic Street target we found is $135. All of them are well below ${{P}}. The exact level depends on our modelling choices; the gap to the price is too large for those choices to close.

  • Holders: reduce or exit, ideally into Nasdaq-100 buying around October 9.
  • No position: avoid buying until the full INTerpath-001 data are public. If the hazard ratio is near 0.5 with an overall-survival trend, revisit using our bull case, which is still about ${{BULL:0}}.
  • Short sellers: the risk is high. The stock moved from $22 to $209 in a year, index inclusion brings forced buying, full data could re-rate it, and Merck could bid for the company to own all of intismeran. Prefer defined-risk structures and small size.

Risks to the Sell call

  • A Merck acquisition. Owning 100% of intismeran is worth more to Merck than to any other buyer, and a takeover premium would land on today's elevated price.
  • Exceptional full data. A large effect with an early overall-survival benefit could lift adoption, pricing and the read-across to lung cancer above our bull case.
  • Platform value we leave out. We value Moderna's early pipeline at zero and charge ongoing platform cost against the listed programs. Each $250M a year of overhead removed adds about $5 per share.
  • Flow and positioning. Index inclusion, short covering and momentum can keep the stock above fundamental value for longer than a 12-month horizon.

Where we differ from the Street

Our target is below every published target. The main reasons: we charge platform and corporate overhead without crediting unlisted programs, we use a 10% discount rate, we price intismeran at about $300K in the US rather than $475K, and we apply standard industry success rates to the eight unread intismeran trials. Even adopting William Blair's $5.4B melanoma peak raises our DCF only to about $29.
